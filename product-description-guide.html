How to write a product description that sells

5 min read

A product description has one job: help the right customer decide to buy. Most descriptions fail because they list features nobody asked about. Here’s a simple structure that sells, with before-and-after examples.

The 4-part structure

  1. Headline with the main benefit. Not “Ceramic mug, 350 ml” but “The mug that keeps your coffee hot through your first meeting.”
  2. One short paragraph for the feeling. Who is it for, and how will their day be better?
  3. 3 benefit bullets. Turn each feature into what it does for the customer.
  4. Remove the last doubt. Size, materials, shipping time, returns — whatever stops people from clicking “buy”.

Example: before and after

Before

Wireless earbuds. Bluetooth 5.3. 40h battery. Noise cancelling. Black.

After

Sound, distilled. Aura Buds strip away the noise so you can focus on what you’re listening to.
• 40-hour battery that outlasts your busiest week
• Noise cancelling for trains, cafés and open offices
• Feather-light fit you’ll forget you’re wearing
Free shipping and 30-day returns.

Turn features into benefits

For every feature, ask “so what?” until you reach something the customer cares about:

  • “Stainless steel” → “won’t rust, even after years in the dishwasher”
  • “1,000 mAh battery” → “charges your phone twice on one battery”
  • “Organic cotton” → “soft on sensitive skin, no itchy tags”
  • “Handmade” → “every piece is slightly different — yours is one of a kind”

Common mistakes

  • Copying the manufacturer’s description (Google sees duplicate text, and so do customers).
  • Writing for everyone instead of one clear customer.
  • Walls of text — use short paragraphs and bullets.
  • Forgetting practical details: dimensions, care, delivery.
